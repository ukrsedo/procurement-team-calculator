Procurement staffing model audit

15 October 2026 · Version 6

Verdict: the numerical workflow is more coherent after corrections and passes the documented checks. The tool remains an exploratory scenario model: effort and waiting-time inputs have not been calibrated on a real procurement department.

Material findings and corrections

FindingWhy it mattersCorrection / treatment
Arrivals stopped after the measurement yearLate-year requests received a quiet period to finish, improving the tail of results.Demand continues during observation. Only middle-year arrivals are measured.
Workforce changes reshuffled incumbent absence schedulesA one-FTE comparison also changed other employees' random sickness and turnover.Stable random streams per position, year and event type. Extra-sickness selection and handover recipients may still legitimately change with the workforce.
Absence episodes could overlapFive ordinary plus ten additional sickness days could become fewer distinct absence days.Vacation, ordinary sickness and additional sickness are scheduled on distinct working days. Vacancy and replacement leave overlap remains an explicit simplifying assumption.
Concurrent handover deductions used a minimumSeveral handovers could consume only the time of one handover.Losses add up, capped at available capacity. Handover remains a capacity reservation, not a detailed transfer-task schedule.
Completed-only P90Excluding unfinished cases could make a struggling team look faster.Incomplete cases rank above observed completions to obtain a conservative P90 upper bound; an unidentifiable bound is withheld.
Daily stage and review timingZero waiting could add a working day, and idle qualified hours could not review work completed later that day.Zero waits can transition the same day. Qualified staff can use actual leftover hours for same-day review; hours are not spent twice.
Separate team queues were implicitOne team could be overloaded while others had capacity, creating a sharp one-FTE cliff.Cross-team support is now selectable. Separate queues remain the default; pooling assumes interchangeable skills.

The one-senior reduction produces a large decline with separate queues because one team loses half its senior staff. With shared workload the effect is much smaller. The allocation policy must match the real operating model; the audit does not establish that all categories can be pooled.

Independent Monte Carlo check

The selected 15-FTE recommendation has 96.6% lowest pooled compliance on 60 search simulations. On 150 independent seeds, lowest pooled compliance is 97.0%, and all-procedure annual target frequency is 77.3%. The corresponding 95% Monte Carlo interval is 70.0%–83.3%.

This is an internal stability check. It is not a historical backtest or external validation.

Verification performed

  • Request conservation, continued observation-period arrivals, no negative daily labour budget, and deterministic seeds.
  • Distinct leave/sickness entitlements; stable incumbent calendars; exact vacancy/ramp calendar boundaries; 10% additional-sickness selection and approximately 5% turnover frequency.
  • Same-day completion with zero waits, impossibility of meeting an SLA shorter than unavoidable waiting, fully overloaded queues and censored P90.
  • 50% senior headcount limit, fractional FTE salary/capacity accounting, cost/SLA objectives and budget limits.
  • Real asynchronous calculations through a DOM interaction harness: FTE changes, main dashboard values, per-procedure SLA, reset, progress, cancellation and invalid staffing.
  • Queue performance optimisation compared against the unoptimised model across 20 cases: all numerical outputs match.

Remaining limits

  • No real demand, effort, external-wait or absence data has been used to calibrate the model.
  • One warm-up year need not reach equilibrium in an overloaded department. Within-day activities, skills, approvals, rework and handovers are simplified.
  • Most staffing shocks are independent. Correlated absence and changing procurement complexity are not estimated.
  • The senior/junior assignments are a fixed policy rather than an optimised task allocation.
  • The search is finite and uses pooled compliance. It does not constrain the probability of meeting every annual SLA; the separate annual-risk output makes that visible.
  • Rendered browser verification was unavailable. Source, numerical and DOM-interaction checks passed.

Use now: compare conditional staffing trade-offs. Do not claim yet: validated real-world staffing requirements or guaranteed SLA performance.
